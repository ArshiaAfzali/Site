Evergreen notes allow you to think about complex ideas by building them up from smaller composable ideas.

My evergreen notes have titles that distill each idea in a succinct and memorable way, that I can use in a sentence. For example:

- A company is a superorganism
- All input is error
- [Calmness is a superpower](https://stephango.com/calmness)
- [Concise explanations accelerate progress](https://stephango.com/concise)
- Cross the chasm
- Everything is a remix
- Writing is telepathy
- You have no obligation to your former self
- etc

You don’t need to agree with the idea for it to become an evergreen note. Evergreen notes can be very short.

I have an evergreen note called **Creativity is combinatory uniqueness** that is built on top of another evergreen note:

> If you believe **Everything is a remix**, then creativity is defined by the uniqueness and appeal of the combination of elements.

Evergreen notes turn ideas into objects. By turning ideas into objects you can manipulate them, combine them, stack them. You don’t need to hold them all in your head at the same time.

Based on the sources provided, Stephan Ango’s approach to writing and extending **evergreen notes** (which appear to be what you referred to as "greennotea") focuses on turning ideas into **"personal memes"** or objects that can be easily manipulated and combined.

Here is the step-by-step approach based on his descriptions:

### 1. Capture "Nuggets" or Memorable Chunks

The first step is to take a feeling, a concept, or a quote and turn it into a **memorable chunk of text**. Ango describes these as "nuggets"—for example, after reading a book about running, he wrote the note **"pain is information"**. These notes are intentionally **subjective** interpretations of ideas rather than objective encyclopedic entries.

### 2. Decompose Ideas into Primitives

You should break your ideas down into the **smallest, most granular pieces** possible. Ango compares these granular notes to **Lego blocks**. By breaking complex problems into these "smaller chunks," you create a stable foundation for building more complex thoughts that you otherwise might not be able to hold in your mind all at once.

### 3. Externalize to Free "Mental RAM"

The act of writing these nuggets down **externalizes** the thought, which is essential because the human brain can typically only hold about seven things in its active memory. Once an idea is a discrete written object, you can "touch," "rotate," and "manipulate" it outside of your head, freeing up mental space to find new combinations.

### 4. Create "Self-Memes" for Fast Reference

The goal is to create a note that acts as a **meme inside your own thinking**. Similar to a scientific citation, a well-crafted evergreen note allows you to reference a complex concept using only a few "tokens" (words). For instance, using the link **"everything is a remix"** within a sentence allows you to bring in a whole pyramid of sub-ideas without having to restate the entire concept.

### 5. Extend and Remix via Linking

Extension happens through **remixing** these fragments. You can use these notes in the context of new sentences, linking them together to see how they interact (e.g., "Because everything is a remix, this thing I found is interesting..."). This **bottom-up organizational model** uses links to create a "web" of ideas similar to a personal Wikipedia.

### 6. Compose in "Thinking Sessions"

While evergreen notes should be granular and have **longevity** (they are not time-stamped), you extend your thinking by having "sessions" where you combine them. During these sessions, you might take ten different evergreen notes and combine them into a **stream-of-consciousness** journal entry or a "thought process". This allows you to explore whether you agree or disagree with your own "memes" and weigh their pros and cons.
